# Estimates and Pricing

## Pricing principles
- Price should reflect labor, prep, materials, equipment, cleanup, and overhead.
- Estimate should clearly separate labor, paint, supplies, and scope assumptions.
- Residential painting jobs usually vary by surface condition, number of coats, difficulty, and access.

## Core estimate categories
- Interior walls and ceilings
- Trim and doors
- Exterior siding, trim, and fascia
- Cabinets and built-ins
- Repair work and drywall patching
- Prime/spot-prime work and stain blocking

## Recommended assumptions
- Always ask whether the customer expects premium-grade paint or value-tier paint.
- Include prep and masking in labor line items.
- Mention if furniture moving, window covering removal, or extensive patching is extra.
- Note if there are lead-safe concerns or environmental restrictions.

## Quote structure
1. Project summary
2. Scope of work
3. Material assumptions
4. Labor breakdown
5. Estimated hours or crew size
6. Total cost
7. Warranty and exclusions

## Delivery best practices
- Give the customer a clean summary and a realistic timeline.
- Use a concise but detailed estimate format.
- Distinguish between included items and optional upgrades.
- Confirm the quote before the crew begins work.
