## Four Core Process Flows

### 1. Lead Capture Flow
**Why it matters:** Speed-to-lead is the single biggest lever on close rate for repaint work.

**Key stages:**
- Inbound lead (phone, web form, referral, neighborhood canvas)
- Qualification check (service type, scope, timeline, geography)
- Schedule estimate appointment (next 24–48 hrs)
- Preparation (crew/materials review, site history if repeat customer)

**Metrics tracked:**
- Time from lead to first contact
- % of leads converted to scheduled estimates
- Repeat customer vs. new customer split

---

### 2. Estimate Flow
**Why it matters:** Pricing is currently hand-calculated, so this maps the human process (not tool logic).

**Key stages:**
- Site walkthrough and measurement
- Specification selection (interior/exterior, prep level, coating, finish)
- Production benchmark calculation (see below)
- Pricing and margin application
- Proposal generation and client presentation
- Follow-up and close (24–72 hr typical window)

**Production Benchmarks (per painter):**
- Spray + back-roll exterior body: 200–300 sq ft/hr
- Brush/roll exterior: 100–150 sq ft/hr
- Interior walls (per coat): 150–200 sq ft/hr
- Prep (includes sanding, caulk, primer): typically 30–40% of total job hours on standard repaint

**Material Specs:**
- Sherwin-Williams Duration spreads ~350–400 sq ft/gal on smooth siding
- Adjust for surface texture (rough siding spreads less)
- Two-coat system standard for interior; one topcoat for exterior refresh

**Pricing logic:**
- Labor: painter-hours × loaded rate (includes taxes, insurance, vehicle, overhead)
- Materials: gallons needed × unit cost + 15% waste factor
- Profit margin: 20–35% target (depends on market, job complexity, repeat customer)

---

### 3. Production & Scheduling Flow
**Why it matters:** Job scheduling determines cash flow and crew utilization.

**Key stages:**
- Job scheduled (customer contract signed, deposit collected if policy requires)
- Pre-job site prep (drop cloths, mask-off, primer if needed)
- Paint application (primary work; crew follows daily checklist)
- Final inspection (touch-ups, cleanup, photos)
- Equipment/vehicle decontamination (if lead-safe protocol in place)

**Crew standards:**
- Daily job logs track start/end time, painter names, square footage completed, material used
- Lead-Safe Crew SOP in effect (RCW 18.27 compliance)
- Quality inspection before close-out (no drips, consistent finish, sealed edges)

---

### 4. Closeout & Retention Flow
**Why it matters:** Most solo/small-crew shops leak cash and reviews by letting the gap between "done" and "invoiced" stretch too long.

**Key stages:**
- Job completion (final checklist signed by crew)
- Invoice generation and delivery (same day or next business day)
- Payment processing (net 30, credit card, ACH)
- Follow-up for testimonial/review (48 hrs post-payment)
- Next service recommendation (touch-ups, additional rooms, seasonal maintenance)
- Retention tracking (repeat customer probability, referral source attribution)

**Timing standards:**
- Invoice issued within 24 hrs of completion
- Payment due net 30 (or specify other terms in contract)
- Testimonial request sent within 48 hrs of payment
- Follow-up contact for next opportunity within 30–60 days

**Retention metrics:**
- % of customers converted to repeat work
- Average customer lifetime value
- Referral source attribution (which jobs lead to most referrals?)
- Review volume and star rating trend

---

## Full Pipeline: Lead Capture → Estimate → Production → Closeout

The four flows connect as a single end-to-end process:

1. **Lead captured** (timing: must respond within 24 hrs)
2. **Estimate delivered** (timing: 3–5 business days typical)
3. **Production scheduled** (timing: 1–4 weeks out depending on seasonality)
4. **Closeout and payment** (timing: invoice issued, payment net 30, testimonial requested)

### Weakest-Link Analysis

**Current priority:** Estimate-to-signed conversion is the stated weakest link (manual pricing process). If this is where leads drop, the next step is to pressure-test the estimate flow timing and margin assumptions against your actual close rate.

**Other potential friction points:**
- Lead-to-estimate scheduling (if backlog is high, speed-to-estimate drops)
- Production quality (if finish quality is inconsistent, retention drops)
- Closeout timing (if invoices lag by days, cash flow suffers)

## How to Use These Flows

1. **For crew training:** Print the PDF and post in job trailer or crew folder
2. **For SOPs:** Reference specific flow stages when documenting procedures
3. **For capacity planning:** Use production benchmarks to estimate crew-hours needed
4. **For pricing:** Cross-reference the estimate flow benchmarks when hand-calculating quotes
5. **For improvement:** Identify which stage is your current bottleneck and run a trial to optimize timing/cost
