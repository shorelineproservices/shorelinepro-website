Frequently Asked Questions

Straight answers about timelines, prep, PNW weather, and how working with us actually goes.

How long does exterior paint last in the Pacific Northwest?

A quality exterior repaint with proper prep typically holds up 7–10 years in Clark County's climate, though direct-sun and south/west-facing walls will fade faster than shaded sides. The Pacific Northwest's wet winters and UV-heavy summers are harder on paint than a drier climate — prep (pressure washing, scraping, priming bare wood, caulking) matters more here than the paint brand itself. Cutting corners on prep is the most common reason a repaint fails early.

Do I need a permit to repaint my house?

No — repainting or restaining an existing structure in its current color scheme generally doesn't require a permit in Vancouver, WA or unincorporated Clark County. If you're in an HOA, though, exterior color changes often need HOA approval first, separate from any city permit question — worth checking your HOA's covenants before committing to a color change.

What's the best time of year to paint in Vancouver, WA?

Late spring through early fall is ideal for exterior work, but it's no longer strictly a summer-only job. Modern weather-adaptive exterior coatings (like Sherwin-Williams Latitude) apply reliably down to 35°F and resist rain within 30 minutes of application, which extends a usable exterior painting window well into fall and early spring — useful if you want work done before peak season books up.

What's the difference between staining and painting a deck or fence?

Stain penetrates or forms a thinner film on the wood and lets the grain show through — it needs reapplication more often (every 2–4 years depending on exposure) but won't peel the way paint can, since it doesn't form a thick surface film. Solid-color stain and paint both fully hide the wood grain and last longer per application, but will eventually need scraping/prep to redo rather than a simple recoat. For decks specifically (a walking surface with more wear), stain is usually the better long-term choice.

How long does interior paint take to dry and cure?

Most interior paints are touch-dry within an hour and safe to recoat within 4 hours, but full cure — the point where the paint reaches its final hardness and washability — takes longer, commonly 2–4 weeks depending on the product. We recommend avoiding heavy scrubbing or pushing furniture against freshly painted walls during that cure window even though the paint looks and feels dry well before then.

Do I need to move my furniture before an interior paint job?

Small and medium furniture pieces should be moved to the center of the room or out entirely if possible — we cover and protect what's left in place, but an emptier room means faster, cleaner work. Large furniture that can't be moved (built-ins, pianos, etc.) is worked around and fully protected.
